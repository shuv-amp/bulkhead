# 12 — Submission

Bulkhead was submitted to the Colosseum Crypto World's Fair (Solana track) on 12 Oct 2026. This page gathers what a reviewer needs in one place: what it is, where each claim can be checked, and what came before the hackathon.

## 1. In brief

- **One line:** the circuit breaker your Solana protocol's own admin can't turn off.
- **Short:** Drift had withdrawal limits; the attacker raised them. Bulkhead holds a protocol's token accounts. Taking back your own deposit is never throttled; any other payout counts against 2% of the vault an hour and 5% a day when it is requested (of a day's requests, at most 10% is paid that day), the rest waits at least 6 hours in a public queue guardians can freeze and cancel, and loosening waits 72h by constant. Floor puts a 72h delay and a guardian stop under the program's upgrade key.
- **Status:** devnet only, not audited, not for real funds (`SECURITY.md`).

## 3. Go-to-market

- **Who pays first:** new and relaunching Solana protocols, then the lending, perps, stablecoin and vault protocols above $10M TVL. Their depositors, curators and STRIDE ask for a worst-case number; admin-controlled limits can't give one.
- **What they pay for:** the core is free and open source. Protocols pay for Bulkhead Cloud: a hosted guardian, calibration on their own flows, a public attestation, and STRIDE evidence ($2.5k–$10k a month, `11`).
- **How we reach them:**
  1. Floor, a one-transaction step any upgradeable program can take, with a free SIMD-0500 readiness check (`tools/floor_check.py`).
  2. Public replays of notable exploits against Bulkhead.
  3. New protocols integrating Vault before launch.
  4. Security firms running guardians for their audit clients.
- **Milestones:** three design partners on devnet in November; a Solana-specialist audit in December; a TVL-capped mainnet partner in Q1 2027 (`11 §8`).

## 4. Prior-code disclosure

- **Repository:** started on 26 Sep 2026, during the hackathon; the design was drafted on 24 Sep 2026.
- **Prior work by the founder, not included:** SP-Differ (https://github.com/shuv-amp/sp-differ), a BIP-352 differential harness. Bulkhead reuses its *method* (independent implementations and a differential runner), not its code.
- **Pattern credited:** the one-transaction abort pattern is Dean Little's `sbpf-asm-abort`. Bulkhead's abort binaries are generated byte by byte by our own code, and an independent Python builder produces the same bytes.
- **Libraries:** Pinocchio (on-chain), LiteSVM (tests), the Solana SDK crates, all Apache-2.0. No third-party code copied (`DISCLOSURE.md`).
- **Third-party data:** Chainalysis, BlockSec, Halborn and Drift (incident facts); Zhou et al., IEEE S&P 2023 (incident statistics); the solgov public API (governance census, snapshot committed in `evidence/`); DefiLlama (market data).
